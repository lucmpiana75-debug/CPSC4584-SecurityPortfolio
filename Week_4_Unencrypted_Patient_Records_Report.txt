# Week 4: Unencrypted Patient Records on a Shared Drive
Course: CPSC 4584 | Special Topics in Information Security
Date: September 21, 2026
Analyst: Nfumu Mpiana
Audit ID: AUD-2026-0921-001

---

## Incident Summary

An audit found 8,247 patient records stored as plaintext on a shared drive with broad read and write access. The records contained sensitive PHI, creating a risk of unauthorized access or exposure.

---

## HIPAA Compliance Assessment

Requirement: Encryption at Rest
Status: REQUIRES REVIEW
Finding: HIPAA requires Maplewood to decide if encryption is reasonable and appropriate and document that decision. Storing PHI in plaintext increases the risk if the files are accessed or copied.

Requirement: Access Controls
Status: CONTROL FAILURE
Finding: Too many authenticated users had read and write access to the patient records instead of access being limited to users who needed it.

Requirement: Audit Controls
Status: CONTROL FAILURE
Finding: The available logs did not provide enough access history to determine who previously viewed or changed the files.

---

## Cryptographic Controls Evaluated

Base64 encoding: Base64 is encoding, not encryption. It changes how information is displayed but does not provide confidentiality because it can easily be decoded.

Caesar cipher: A Caesar cipher hides information by shifting letters, but it is outdated and easy to break. It does not provide modern cryptographic protection.

Modern encryption at rest: Maplewood should evaluate a recognized encryption method such as AES for stored ePHI. This would make the information unreadable without the correct encryption key if someone obtained the files.

---

## Hashing Commands Practiced

Command: echo -n "..." | sha256sum
Purpose: Demonstrated that SHA-256 creates a repeatable digest and that a small change in the input produces a different hash.
Output Length: 64 hexadecimal characters

Command: echo -n "..." | md5sum
Purpose: Demonstrated the difference between MD5 and SHA-256. MD5 is weaker because it does not provide strong collision resistance.
Output Length: 32 hexadecimal characters

Command: sha256sum .bashrc
Purpose: Demonstrated how a file hash can be recorded before and after copying a file to verify that its contents did not change.
Output Length: 64 hexadecimal characters

---

## Escalation Summary

The audit confirmed that 8,247 patient records containing sensitive PHI were stored without encryption and had overly broad access permissions. The retained logs cannot show who previously accessed the files, so past access cannot be fully reconstructed. These findings should be reported to leadership, security, privacy, and legal teams to review whether a breach occurred and decide what safeguards or remediation are needed. As a Tier 1 analyst, I should report the evidence and not make the final decision that a HIPAA breach occurred.
